# Answering screening questions honestly and consistently

The answer to every factual question comes from master.md or answers.md. This file covers the question types that cause trouble.

## Work authorization and sponsorship

Questions are phrased differently but mean the same thing. Map them consistently:
- "Are you legally authorized to work in <country>?": the truth for that country (usually "No" for a candidate who needs sponsorship).
- "Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?": "Yes" if they need it.
- "Do you have the right to work in <country>?" with options like "I require sponsorship": pick that option.
- "Are you currently based in <country>?": the truth. Then use any free-text field to add "Relocating, able to start after <notice> plus visa processing."

Never answer "Yes" to authorization to pass a filter. It wastes everyone's time and can end an offer late in the process.

## Years of experience

- Take the number from the skills table in master.md. Never round up.
- Platforms often pre-fill these fields from the CV, sometimes wrongly. Correct the pre-filled value, and mention that you did in the log notes.
- If a field only allows whole numbers and the true value is 3.5, enter 3.

## Salary

Follow the salary rule in answers.md. A good default rule:
1. Free-text field: "Open, aligned with market rate for the role and location."
2. Required number and the posting shows a band: a figure inside the band, around the lower-middle.
3. Required number and no band: a market median for the role, seniority and city, in the currency and period the form asks for (monthly vs yearly matters).
4. If the candidate gave a hard floor, never go below it.

Log the figure you used.

## Notice period and start date

From answers.md. For relocation roles, add visa processing time when asked about earliest start ("about 30 days notice plus visa processing").

## Address

Forms that insist on a street address get the candidate's real current address as configured in answers.md. If a form insists on a local address in the target country that the candidate does not have, skip the job and log why.

## Demographic and diversity questions

Use the candidate's configured preference from answers.md (an actual answer or "Prefer not to say"). These questions are voluntary almost everywhere.

## Consent checkboxes

Privacy-policy or data-processing consent required to submit an application is normally within the candidate's authorization to apply. Optional marketing consent: leave unticked. Anything that reads like a contract, payment agreement or legal declaration beyond the application: hand off to the human.

## "Have you used our product?" and personal-opinion questions

These ask about the candidate's own experience. If master.md or answers.md does not cover it, hand off. Do not invent a personal experience.

## Long-form questions ("Why us?", "Describe a hard problem")

- Build the answer from the stories bank in master.md, adapted to the company.
- Follow the writing-style rules in answers.md. Short, specific, in the candidate's voice.
- If the question needs an opinion or experience the candidate has not given you, hand off.

## AI-usage questions

Some forms ask how the candidate uses AI tools, or ask them to confirm answers were not AI-written. Answer only from what the candidate has stated. If a form asks for a personal declaration about authorship, hand it to the candidate.

## Questions with no stored answer

Leave the field, mark the job "Needs <name>", record the exact question, continue to the next job. When the candidate answers, add the answer to answers.md so it is never asked again.
